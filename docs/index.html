The strategy

What it does

  • A dashboard that recommends stocks from the S&P 500 and Nasdaq-100 based on their expected earnings growth (EPS growth - G) and valuation vs EPS growth (PEG).
  • It buys a stock when it passes two tests in each of the next 2–3 years: an EPS growth test (expected growth of at least about 15% a year) and a PEG test (P/E ÷ growth low enough compared with similar companies), with forecasts from at least 10 analysts.
  • It sells when either test fails in any of the coming 2 years: growth forecasts drop, or the price runs ahead so the PEG gets too high.
  • It holds up to 10 stocks, each in its own slot of money. A stronger new stock replaces the holding with the least room left (lowest 12m upside), and each quarter every holding with less than 10% upside left is sold, taking profit where little room is left.
  • Recommendations are made within 1–2 weeks after each quarterly earnings report, when analysts publish their new EPS forecasts.

How it performed in tests

  • 2008–2022 (15 years): beat QQQ by about 14% a year, with a worst fall 24 points smaller (−22% vs −46%): when few stocks qualified, the empty slots sat in cash. It shone in 2022: flat while QQQ fell 33%, because it had already left expensive tech.
  • Since 2023 the lead grew: about 39% a year ahead of QQQ and 38% a year ahead of QGRW (an ETF built on a similar growth idea), with a worst fall 4–5 points smaller (−19% vs −23% / −24%). It rode the AI and memory-chip profit boom (Nvidia, then Seagate, Western Digital, Micron). The one soft spot: 2023 itself, when it trailed QQQ’s sharp rebound (+34% vs +55%).
  • These figures already use the 10 fixed slots and assume it only buys or sells about 3 trading days after each report, once analysts have updated (waiting costs about 3–4% a year).

Things to be aware of

  • Past analyst forecasts are not available, so the tests used companies’ actual later profits with a 30% random error as the “forecast”. Even with 50% random errors the results stayed far ahead of QQQ. The bigger risk is analysts being wrong all together, for example all missing a boom or a bust, which the tests cannot fully capture.
  • Real analyst forecasts are now saved every day (forecast history, free to download), so this assumption can be checked against real results as fiscal years are reported.
  • Timing: most analysts update their forecasts within days of a report (about 80% of stocks within 2 weeks), so the dashboard picks up most changes in time. Share prices move faster: by the time forecasts are raised, the share is already about 2.5% ahead of the market (about 3% behind when cut). The tests allow for this.
  • Holds up to 10 shares, mostly tech: expect bigger swings than an index fund.

How to use it

  • BUY qualifies now · HOLD owned and still qualifies · SELL growth outlook faded · WATCH nearly there.
  • Act on stocks tagged “review now” (reported 3–14 days ago, forecasts settled). A “pending” tag next to BUY or SELL means the trade has not happened yet. Hover it to see exactly what it is pending, e.g. the next quarterly report (and its date), the first close of next month, or analysts still updating their forecasts.
  • Upside (12m target) shows how much room may be left. It is not a sell target, but holdings with little upside left make way for stronger new buys, and below 10% they are sold at the quarterly review.
  • Every S&P 500 and Nasdaq-100 stock is listed with its earnings history, growth forecasts and 12m target (Signals table and Stock detail), so you can look up any stock even when it is not picked.
  • Tags such as “few analysts” or “one-off qtr” flag numbers worth a second look. A research tool, not investment advice.
Universe
  • S&P 500 + Nasdaq-100; a stock is bought only while it is a member.
Terms
  • G = expected EPS growth per year: current, next and year-after fiscal years (and the last 12 months for buys).
  • P/E = price ÷ EPS of the last 4 reported quarters.
  • PEG = P/E ÷ G in %.
  • Sector P/E = median P/E of the stock’s sector in the S&P 500 / Nasdaq-100.
  • Market P/E = median P/E of the whole universe; its 2002–26 average is 19.
Buy: all of these, in every forecast year and over the last 12 months
  • G ≥ 15% × market P/E ÷ 19 (EPS growth rule)
  • PEG ≤ sector P/E ÷ 30 (PEG rule)
  • At least 10 analysts forecasting next fiscal year (otherwise WATCH)
Sell: any of these
  • In any forecast year, G < 10% × market P/E ÷ 19 or PEG > sector P/E ÷ 25 (growth forecasts drop, or the price runs ahead)
  • All slots full and a stock with ≥ 5 points more 12m upside needs the slot (the lowest-upside holding is replaced)
  • First close of each quarter: 12m upside < 10%
  • The last, already reported year is shown but not used.
Timing
  • Decisions 3–14 days after each stock’s report, or at the first close of each month (monthly sweep).
  • An empty slot is filled at once, any day, with the waiting BUY with the most 12m upside.
  • Only when the stock’s data is ready: forecasts settled (≤ 10% of analysts revised next year in 7 days, or consensus moved < 1%; decided anyway from day 12); fiscal year rolled over at Yahoo; ≥ 3 next-year forecasts; year-after forecasts aligned; data fresh. Otherwise the page shows “pending”.
  • Holdings that leave the indexes stay tracked until the rules sell them; a delisted or acquired holding is sold at its last close.
Portfolio
  • 10 slots of equal start size; each stock keeps its own money (no trims, no rebalancing).
  • Sale: the whole slot goes to the next qualifying stock – at once if a BUY is already waiting, otherwise it waits in cash.
  • All slots full: a new buy replaces the holding with the lowest 12m upside if its own upside is ≥ 5 points higher.
  • First close of each quarter: every holding with < 10% upside is sold.
12m target
  • Expected 12-month peak = last close × (1 + forecast next-12m EPS growth) × P/E-model change.
  • Upside = target ÷ price − 1: a guide to the room left, not a sell price.
  • Used for swaps and the quarterly sale (upside < 10%).
